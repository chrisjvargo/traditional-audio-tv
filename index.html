Audio · measurement

Diaries, meters, and the tyranny of the quarter-hour

How Nielsen Audio counts

  • PPM (Portable People Meter) markets: panelists carry meters that detect encoded station audio — passive, granular, expensive; top markets only.
  • Diary markets: respondents write listening in quarter-hours from memory — cheaper, recall-biased toward favorite stations and round times.
  • Currency metrics: AQH (average quarter-hour persons), Cume (weekly reach), rating = AQH ÷ population. Dayparts (morning drive 6–10a etc.) are the buying units.

What to interrogate

  • Sample size per demo cell: a "spike" in women 25–34 can be eleven diaries.
  • Metro vs. total survey area: which geography is the rating actually describing?
  • Streaming simulcast included or not? Total-line reporting changes the station's number without changing a single listener.
Same lesson, third medium

Geopath modeled movement; Nielsen samples people. Both publish confident decimals. Your job is to know which ritual produced the number before you price it.

Speaker notes

Denver-Boulder is a PPM market — local texture available. Host-read/endorsement spots and sports play-by-play are radio's trust formats; flag for creative section.

Audio · the buy

Spot radio: cheap reach, brutal frequency, local voices

  • Units: :60/:30/:15 spots in daypart rotators or fixed positions; drive times price at a premium because the car is captive (83% share, remember).
  • Economics: local spot CPMs routinely sit far below digital video — radio buys frequency against a geography (dealers, QSR, services, political) better than almost anything.
  • Formats are audiences: news/talk, sports, country, CHR, Spanish-language — format selection is targeting, with host trust as the contextual layer (your Peer39 lesson, human edition).
  • Digital extension: station streams, podcasts, and network audio (Westwood One, iHeart) sell alongside broadcast — one buy, measured in two currencies. Reconcile before reporting, like Subaru tabs.

Television · local

Local TV: news, sports, weather — and every election

  • The product: local news dayparts (morning, early fringe, late news), syndication, network prime/sports adjacencies, sold by DMA through station groups — Nexstar, Gray, Sinclair, TEGNA, Scripps — and rep firms.
  • The political engine: AdImpact's 2026 forecast puts ~$5.6B (just under 50% of political spend) into broadcast, ahead of CTV ($2.7B), digital ($1.6B), cable ($1.4B), radio ($300M). In battleground DMAs, Q4 inventory effectively re-prices for everyone — including your car dealer.
  • Why it persists: geographic precision without identity data, older-skewing high-propensity audiences (voters, homeowners), live/local trust, and co-op dealer money that must be spent locally.
Planning consequence

In even-numbered falls, non-political local advertisers get preempted and priced out of news adjacencies. A capstone plan with local TV in Oct–Nov of an election year needs a preemption makegood clause and a CTV/radio pressure valve — write it before the station does.

Speaker notes

Kagan estimate for local OTA political was ~$3.8–4.2B (narrower definition than AdImpact's broadcast total) — use the gap itself to teach forecast definitions. Colorado 2026 races made Denver inventory tight this fall; students saw the density firsthand.

Worked skeleton (illustrative)

Denver DMA adults 25–54 ≈ 1.4M. A 300-TRP local news flight at $450 CPP costs $135K for ≈4.2M target impressions (CPM ≈ $32). Expensive per thousand vs. Subaru display ($3.14) — and it reaches cord-cut households your DSP never sees, in trusted live context, by Friday. Price is meaningless without the reach-completion job.

Measurement honesty

Nielsen panels + big-data return-path blends are estimates with sample error, exactly like diaries. Local demos get thin fast. Report ranges and universes, not false decimals.

Convergence, honestly

"Traditional" is now a buying style, not a technology

  • Radio groups sell audio portfolios: broadcast + streams + podcasts + smart-speaker inventory in one proposal, often with digital attribution add-ons. The station rep is now an audio consultant with a dashboard.
  • Local TV sells impressions beyond the spot: station OTT/CTV apps, addressable overlays, and extended-reach digital bundled with linear schedules. Your Week 9 CTV skills apply — with DMA geography instead of device graphs.
  • Programmatic pipes reach both: audio exchanges and TV platforms (e.g., station-group programmatic marketplaces) trade on ratings-informed impressions. Peter's partnerships world again — guaranteed local deals inside national pipes.
The planner's synthesis

Buy the job (drive-time frequency, live-news trust, election-proof reach), then choose the pipe (direct spot, network, programmatic). Medium-first planning is how budgets inherit last year's shape.

Speaker notes

Warn on double counting when broadcast + stream audiences are summed in proposals — the Subaru tabs lesson in a new costume. Ask for deduplicated reach or label it gross.
